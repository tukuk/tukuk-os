TITLE: Escape the Algorithm — How to Discover Music and Radio on Your Own Terms
EXCERPT: Recommendation engines optimise for the next hour of your attention, not for the song you will love for a decade. Here is the case for deliberate discovery — live radio, human-programmed stations, and searching instead of being served — and how to start.
TAGS: music, radio, discovery, algorithm, streaming, culture

## What the algorithm is actually for

Every major music platform opens with a feed of "recommended for you". It is genuinely good at one thing: guessing what will keep you listening *right now*. It learns by giving you a little of what you already proved you like, measuring how long you stay, and repeating. That loop converges — it narrows you into a comfortable trench of songs that resemble the last songs. Nothing pushes you toward the strange album, the foreign station, the genre you cannot yet name, because those carry a real chance you skip and leave. The algorithm optimises engagement; you probably wanted discovery. Those are different goals.

## The oldest discovery tool is a live broadcast

Internet radio is the antidote that was already here. A live station is programmed by a **human** who decided, hours ago, what plays next — and crucially, it is playing *now*, for everyone, whether or not you asked. Tune in and you get records you would never have selected, sequenced by someone with taste and context. It is also, for what it's worth, one of the most low-bandwidth things you can stream: a single continuous audio feed, no image assets, works fine on a weak connection. Tukuk's [world radio directory](https://tukuk.org/radio) collects stations from many countries so the default is "somewhere you have never been", not "more of last week".

## Deliberate habits that beat the feed

- **Search instead of scroll.** Open the [music explorer](https://tukuk.org/music) or the [search engine](https://tukuk.org/) with a concrete thread to pull: a country's scene, a session musician, a year, a label. Curiosity with a target beats an infinite feed.
- **Pick one non-home station a day.** Randomness from a live broadcaster is cheaper and more surprising than personalisation.
- **Follow the humans.** A specific DJ, a public-radio music desk, a single record label's roster. A person's taste is a *curated queue* you did not have to train.
- **Let news and culture cross-pollinate.** Discovery is not only audio — browsing the [news feed](https://tukuk.org/news) for what a scene is doing this month often surfaces artists no algorithm would link to your history.

## Why this fits a no-tracking web

Personalisation only works by collecting a profile of you. Choosing discovery-by-search and live radio instead means no watch-history is needed to enjoy something good — the same principle behind everything on [tukuk.org](https://tukuk.org): free, account-free, and honest about the trade-off between being *served* and being *curious*. The next song you love for a decade is almost always outside the trench. Go look for it yourself.

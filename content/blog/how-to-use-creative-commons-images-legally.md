## The mistake almost everyone makes

Right-clicking an image and saving it feels free, so people assume it *is* free. Legally, almost every photograph, illustration, and graphic is **copyrighted the moment it is made** — the default is "all rights reserved", not "no rights reserved". Reposting it on a blog, a slide deck, or a product page without permission is infringement even if you did not profit and even if you had no idea. The internet runs on a layer of media that was explicitly set free for reuse — but you have to know which layer you are standing on.

## The licenses that actually let you reuse

**Creative Commons (CC)** is a family of standard licenses that let a creator say "yes, reuse this, with these conditions." The pieces combine:

- **CC0 / Public Domain** — no conditions at all. Use it, edit it, sell prints, no credit needed. The safest category.
- **CC BY** — free to reuse and adapt, even commercially, as long as you **attribute** the author.
- **CC BY-SA** — like BY, but your new work must carry the same license (the "share-alike" rule Wikipedia uses).
- **CC BY-NC** — non-commercial only. The moment your page has ads or sells something, be careful.
- **CC BY-ND** — no derivatives: you may repost it but not crop or edit it.

The two that trip people up are **NC** (commercial use banned) and **SA** (your remix inherits obligations). Read the exact letters, not the phrase "free image".

## Attribution that is actually correct

Good attribution is not a watermark or a guess. Keep it simple and near the image: **title, author, source link, and license name/link**. One honest line does it: *"『Dusk over the harbour』by A. Photographer, CC BY 2.0."* That satisfies most CC-BY terms and shows a reader you did not claim the work. If the source offers a machine-readable credit string, copy it verbatim rather than paraphrasing.

## Where to look for genuinely free media

Rather than reverse-image-searching a stranger's photo, start from repositories built for reuse. Tukuk's [Creative Commons search](https://tukuk.org/cc?q=mountains) surfaces Wikimedia Commons images and returns each result with its **license and author** baked in, so you know the terms before you download, not after a complaint. Search a term, filter, and reuse with the supplied credit. When a project demands zero obligations, narrow to CC0/public-domain sources.

## The one-line rule

If you cannot find a license that says you may use it, assume you may not. The right habit is: **find free-licensed media first, read the letters, and attribute once.** Do those three and the whole "is this allowed?" anxiety disappears — and the same respect for creators is the reason tools on [tukuk.org](https://tukuk.org) prefer open, no-tracking sources by default.

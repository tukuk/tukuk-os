## Formats that do not match

iPhone cameras output **HEIC**, but websites want **JPG**. Your voice recording is a **WAV** but too big to send — you want an **MP3**. A video needs shortening into a **GIF** to paste into a post. These trivial problems usually need special software or an upload site that demands registration (and sometimes sneaks in a watermark). A good **browser format converter** settles it in one tab.

## How it works

Pick a file, choose the target format, convert. Prefer tools that process **on your side** (in the browser, via WebCodecs/WASM for many cases) — your file never has to go to a remote server, which is more private and often faster for medium-sized files. This covers images (JPG/PNG/WebP), shrinking sizes, and basic audio/video conversion.

## Why "no install" matters

Non-technical people do not want to install ffmpeg or converter apps that clutter the desktop. They just want *one file in another format*. With a browser tool, the barrier disappears: open the link, do the work, close it. Many such pages can also be **added to your home screen** as a PWA, so the converter is one tap away next time.

## Quick format cheatsheet

- **Images**: JPG for photos, PNG for transparency/text, WebP to save size.
- **Audio**: MP3 for sharing, AAC for better quality at the same bitrate, WAV only when you must keep the original.
- **Video**: MP4/H.264 for the widest compatibility; WebM for the web.

TITLE: "Planets Are Aligning and the World Will End" — What an Alignment Actually Is
EXCERPT: Every few years a viral post claims a rare planetary alignment will trigger earthquakes or doom. Here is what the planets really do, in geometry you can check with your own eyes.
TAGS: planets, astronomy, myths, space, science

## What People Mean by "Alignment"

The planets never form a line through space. What "planetary alignment" always means, in every viral post that has ever circulated, is **a sharing of ecliptic longitude**: seen from the Sun, the planets spread out within a slice of sky — say, within 30 or 60 degrees — rather than being scattered all around the 360-degree circle. They remain at wildly different distances from each other and from the Sun, and the slice they occupy is a two-dimensional projection, the same as calling two cities "in a line" because both sit on the highway, no matter how far apart.

Because the planets orbit at different speeds — Mercury in 88 days, Neptune in 165 years — this kind of loose clustering happens constantly. Several times a year there is a stretch of sky containing three or four naked-eye planets. Astronomers call it a conjunction, or a "planet parade" when five or six gather near the sunrise or sunset glow. It is lovely; it is not rare.

## Why a Line Is Geometrically Impossible

The planets' orbits are tilted a few degrees relative to Earth's (Mercury by 7 degrees, Pluto's orbit by 17 — and Pluto is not a planet in the count anyway). A true straight line through the Sun and several planets requires the planets to cross the ecliptic plane at exactly the right moment, in exactly the right order. The odds of any *significant* alignment of many bodies are so small that the solar system has never done it in its 4.6 billion years in the form the doomsday posts describe — and when posts claim "the last alignment was in year X, the next is in year Y," the dates are usually invented, because no ephemeris service ever issued such a prediction.

## The Gravity Question, Answered Properly

The claim: "if the planets line up, their combined gravity will crack the Earth / trigger earthquakes." Do the arithmetic once and it is dead forever:

- The **tides** an astronomical body raises on Earth scale with its mass divided by the cube of its distance. The Moon, tiny but close, dominates; the Sun's tides are about half the Moon's; every other planet contributes a trickle.
- Jupiter, at its absolute closest possible approach to Earth, raises tides on our planet that are a tiny fraction — on the order of hundred-thousandths — of the Moon's. All the other planets combined are smaller still.
- And the "alignment" geometry makes it worse for the claim, not better: even in a conjunction, the inner planets sit on *different sides* of the Sun most of the time, so their pulls partly cancel.

No credible geophysical study has ever linked alignments to earthquakes; the correlation studies that tried found nothing, and earthquake prediction by planetary position is not practised by any seismological agency on Earth. The Sun and Moon's own gravity — which genuinely flexes the crust every single day, twice a day — does not set earthquakes off in any measurable rhythm. If the Moon's far stronger daily flexure is not the trigger, Jupiter's whisper certainly is not.

## What to Watch Instead (This Month, Any Month)

Conjunctions are gifts, not threats. A handful of permanent, checkable sky events:

- **Two bright planets close together at dusk or dawn** — Venus and Jupiter meet in celestial longitude roughly every eight months, and on those occasions that they also match distance in the twilight — as in the spectacular pairs of 2020 and 2022 — they light up social media for a week.
- **The Moon passing in front of a planet** (an occultation) — the Moon crosses every planet's path month after month; during a planet's visible season it periodically sails directly in front, an event exact enough to predict to the minute and watchable with binoculars from the right strip of Earth.
- **Five-planet parades** — visible to the naked eye near sunrise or sunset whenever Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn all rise within the same window; the last widely publicised ones happened with no catastrophe beyond a shortage of phone batteries.
- **Transits and oppositions** — a planet crossing the Sun's face (Mercury transits every few years in May/November), or a planet directly opposite the Sun, rising all night at its biggest and brightest.

All of these are computed from orbits that have been known for three centuries. If a headline about the sky needs your fear to work, it is lying; if it needs your *binoculars*, it is astronomy.

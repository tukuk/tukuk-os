## Why Tricks Beat Rote Drilling

Every trick below works because it moves the calculation to a friendlier shape — round numbers, known facts, smaller steps. Working memory is the bottleneck in mental arithmetic, not intelligence; these techniques shrink the number of things you must hold at once. And because each one has a one-line algebraic reason, learning *why* it works is what makes you fast — memorised procedures rot, understood ones don't.

## 1. Compensation: Move to the Nearest Round Number

**47 + 38** → round 38 up to 40: 47 + 40 = 87, then take back the 2 you over-added = **85**.
Any addition: round one term, then correct. Subtraction the same way: 83 − 29 = 83 − 30 + 1 = **54** (you removed one too many, so give one back — the sign of the correction is the only thing to remember).
*Why it works: adding and subtracting the same amount does not change the result.*

## 2. Left-to-Right Instead of School Order

School algorithms start from the units column; spoken numbers start from the largest part. Add **356 + 742** as 300+700=1000, then 50+40=1050, then 6+2=**1058**. Say the growing number aloud and the answer arrives already in speaking order — which is why this feels faster than the carried-column method you were taught.
*Why it works: place value is just addition of parts; you are free to add the big parts first.*

## 3. Doubling and Halving (the Multiplication Skeleton Key)

**16 × 35** → halve and double keeps the product: 16×35 = 8×70 = 4×140 = **560**.
It shines when one factor is even and the other is ugly: 14 × 45 = 7 × 90 = **630**.
*Why it works: (a×b) = (a/2)×(2b). You are allowed to halve one thing and double the other, forever.*

## 4. The ×9 Shortcut

9 × anything = 10 × it − itself. **9 × 47 = 470 − 47 = 423.** Do it as "four hundred and seventy, minus forty-seven" and most people land on 423 in two seconds.
*Why it works: 9 = 10 − 1; distributive law, zero memorisation.*

## 5. Squaring Any Number That Ends in 5

**65²** → take the tens digit (6), multiply by its successor (6×7 = 42), append **25** → **4225**.
Try 85²: 8×9 = 72 → **7225**. Try 115²: 11×12 = 132 → **13225**. This never fails.
*Why it works: (10n+5)² = 100n(n+1) + 25. The n(n+1) part fills the hundreds-and-above; the 25 is the residue.*

## 6. Percentages Through the 10% Door

**18% of 50** → of all places, percentages of 50 are the easiest: x% of 50 is just x ÷ 2, so the answer is **9**.
General recipe: find 10% (shift the decimal one place), then assemble. **15% of 80**: 10% = 8, 5% = half of that = 4, total **12**. Tip-calculation in the wild is this and nothing more.
*Why it works: 10% is a decimal-point shift, and every other percentage is a small combination of tens and halves.*

## 7. Estimate Before You Compute — the Sanity Check

Round everything, get the ball park, *then* do the precise work: is 48 × 27 "a bit over 1000" (50×20=1000, 50×7=350 → about 1350) — the real answer **1296** lands inside the bubble. Anyone who estimates first can catch their own slips; anyone who doesn't is one keypress from confident nonsense. This is the single highest-value habit on the page, and schools mostly skip it.
*Why it works: rounding errors are boundable — you always know which way the bias went.*

## How to Actually Get Fast

Speed is a memory effect, not a talent effect. Pick one trick a day, use it for every calculation you meet for a week — shop totals, distances, splitting bills — and the pattern will automate on its own. The gate that matters is *choosing* the right move in the first second; after a few weeks of deliberate use, that choice stops feeling like a choice.
